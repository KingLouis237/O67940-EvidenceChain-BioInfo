=== Biochemical characterization of SalL ===
Chemical complementation of the salL − mutant strain with 5′-ClDA selectively restored SalA production ( Fig. 1c ), thereby confirming the metabolic intermediacy of 5′-ClDA, in which the chlororibose unit gives rise to the ‘chlorobutyrate’ PKS building block in SalA, presumably via the new metabolite chloroethylmalonyl coenzyme A (10) ( Fig. 1a ). Biochemical characterization of SalL as a 5′-ClDA synthase SalL behaves as a soluble (60 mg l −1 ) trimeric protein (30 kDa per monomer of 283 residues) when purified from Escherichia coli BL21(DE3). In the presence of SAM, recombinant SalL showed a surprising flexibility for the halides chloride, bromide and iodide, producing halogenated 5′-deoxyadenosine (5′-XDA) and l -methioine. Notably, no halogenase activity was detected in the presence of fluoride using either standard assays or coupled-enzyme assays in which coproduced l -methionine was oxidized with l -amino acid oxidase to inhibit the reverse reaction 11 . Apparent steady state kinetic constants of SalL were measured in vitro by monitoring the formation of either 5′-XDA (forward reaction) or SAM (reverse reaction) ( Table 1 ). Although the affinity of SalL for SAM ( K m 1 µM) is much higher than that of chloride ( K m 45 mM), seawater has a chloride concentration of 546 mM. The brominase and iodinase activities are probably not biologically relevant in this marine bacterium given the low concentration of these halides in seawater (0.8 mM bromide and 0.5 µM iodide, which are 187 and 520,000 times less than K m , respectively). Replacement of synthetic sea salt with sodium bromide in the S. tropica fermentation medium results in the in vivo production of bromosalinosporamide (11) 12 , which further illuminates the broad halide promiscuity of the sal PKS in accepting a range of biosynthetic building blocks.
