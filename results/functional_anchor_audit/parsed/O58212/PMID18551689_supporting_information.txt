1. Purification of S-Adenosyl-L-methionine:hydroxide adenosyltransferase
The clone containing ORF PH0463 from the marine archaea P. horikoshii  OT3  was purchased from  NBRC
(NITE-DOB, Japan) (NBRC no: NBRC G01 -000-971, Clone ID: S2_2219, EMBL accession No. BAA29549).
The PH0463 ORF was subcloned into pET28a(+) vector (Novagen) using the following two primers: PH0463-
F(GCAGGAGGAATTCATATGATAACCTTAACCACCGACTTCGG) and PH0463-R:
(CGCCGCTCGAGTCATAGCAACCTCACCCTTAGCTC)  with restriction sites NdeI and XhoI, respectively.
The resultant plasmid pET -PH0463 was transferred into E coli BL21 C43(DE3) competent cell and grown in
Luria broth containing kanamycin (100 µg/ml) at 37 OC until an absobance of 0.6 at 600 nm was reached. The
PH0463 protein was over -expressed in E. coli by adding IPTG (1mM) and cells were left to grow at 16 0C for
16 h. Cells were then harvested by centrifugation and were subject to sonication to induce lysis. The cell-free
extract with PBS and imidazole (10 mM) was then centrifuged twice at 20,000g at 4 0C for 15 min. The
supernatant was collected by passing through a Ni-affinity column (Qiagen) and the active fractions were eluted
by adding PBS buffer with imid azole (100 mM). The eluent was dialysed for 16h at 25 oC by adding thrombin
(0.5 unit; Sigma -Aldrich Co. Ltd., ) and adenosine deaminase (0.01 unit, Sigma -Aldrich Co. Ltd.,). The
dialysate was then passed through a Ni -affinity column for a second time and t he eluent was subjected then to
size exclusion chromatography (Size Exclusive column -Amersham Co). The active fractions were collected
and subjected to enzymatic assay.

3. GC-MS method for measurement for the incorporation of the 18O-isotope from [18O]-water into [5'-18O]-
adenosine.

The enzyme (10 µM, 60 µL) was incubated with H 218O (95 atom%, Sigma -Aldrich Co., 100 µL) and an aliquot
(10 µL) of a 20mM SAM solution in PBS buffer at 37 oC for 16 h. After addition of ethanol (500 μL) the solution
was freeze-dried and the  residue was treated with an excess of N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl) trifluoroacetamide
and then heated for 60 min at 100 °C. GC-MS analysis was performed on an Agilent 5890 GC instrument which
was directly attached to Agilent 5973A mass selective detector (M SD). The GC was equipped with an Ultra 1
fused-silica capillary column (Agilent Technologies; 12m × 0.25mm × 0.17 μm). The oven temperature was
programmed to hold for 1 min at 100°C and then ramp at 10°C/min to 300°C. The injector and transfer line
temperatures were set at 250°C and the per -trimethylsilylated sample (1μl) automatically injected in the splitless

4. MS-MS methods
The gel band was excised and cut into 1mm cubes.  These were then subjected to in -gel digestion, using a
ProGest Investigator in -gel digestion robot (Genomic Solutions, Ann Arbor, MI) using standard protocols.
Briefly the gel cubes were destained by washing with acetonitrile and subjected to reduction and alkylation before
digestion with trypsin (36 ng) at 37°C. The peptides were extracted with 10% formic acid and concentrated down
to 20 μL using a SpeedVac (ThermoSavant).

For analysis by nanoLC -ESI MS -MS, they were then separated using an UltiMate nanoLC (LC Packings,
Amsterdam) equipped with a PepMap C 18 trap & column, using a 30 min gradient of increasing acetonitrile
concentration, containing 0.1 % formic acid (5 -35% acetonitrile in 18 min, 35 -50% in a further 7, followed by
95% acetonitrile to clean the column).  The eluent was sprayed into a Q -Star Pulsar XL tandem mass
spectrometer (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA) and analysed in Information Dependent Acquisition (IDA)
mode. MS/MS data for doubly and triply charged precursor ions was converted to centroid data, without
smoothing, using the Masc ot Daemon 2.1 (Matrix Science, London) data import filter for Sciex Analyst.  The
MS/MS data file generated was analysed using the Mascot search engine against an internal database containing
the predicted sequence. The data was searched with tolerances of  0.2 Da for the precursor and fragment ions,
trypsin as the cleavage enzyme, one missed cleavage, carbamidomethyl modification of cysteines as a fixed
modification and methionine oxidation (and 18O incorporation where appropriate) selected as a variable
modification.

For MALDI MS and MS-MS, the sample solution (0.5 μL) was applied to the MALDI target along with α-cyano-
4-hydroxycinnamic acid matrix  (0.5 μL, 10 mg/mL in 50:50 acetonitrile:0.1% TFA).  MALDI MS and MSMS
was acquired using a 4800 MALDI TOF/T OF Analyser (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA) equipped with a
Nd:YAG 355 nm laser and calibrated using a mixture of peptides.  The data was processed as described above
with tolerances of 100 ppm for the precursor and 0.5 Da for the fragment ions.
